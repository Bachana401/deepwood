## Logline
A fallen god-king, hollowed into a nameless mortal, hunts an evil he cannot remember losing to — and finds it wearing the mask of a hero.

---

## The Age of Monarchs (the deep backstory)
Before Deepwood, before the ruin of the world, three great powers ruled — the **Monarchs**:

- The **Monarch of Despair** — who unmakes the living, leaving them neither alive nor dead.
- The **Monarch of War** — whose domain now lies in another realm entirely.
- The **Shadow Monarch** — a power of shadow and death, equal to the other two. **This is the player.**

Ages ago, Despair and War **allied against the Shadow Monarch** and defeated him in a battle that broke the world. Dying, the Shadow Monarch cast one last forbidden spell: he unwove his own power and poured his soul into a mortal shell to survive. It worked — but the price was everything. His throne, his power, his name, and all his memories were **sealed away inside an ordinary human** who woke with nothing.

Since that day the Monarch of War has withdrawn into a different dimension _(deliberately off-screen — the hook for a future game, not part of Deepwood's story)_. The Monarch of Despair went on alone, and turned his hunger on mankind.

## The World Now
The Monarch of Despair has **killed half of humanity and razed cities and whole countries.** He does not simply slay — he drains the living and leaves them frozen between breath and grave, so his harvest never ends. Survivors know the calamity only by what it does to a soul: **despair.**

## The One Who Comes (the player)
You are the Shadow Monarch — but you don't know it, and neither does the game tell you. You are **a nameless, blank mortal**: no fixed history, no face the story paints, deliberately empty so the player steps straight into the role. For mortal lifetimes you have wandered as a hunter, pulled toward the evil by an instinct you can't explain, killing its lesser servants, never reaching the thing itself.

**Your true nature and memories return only when you reclaim your Shadow Monarch form — at the very end.** Until then you are just a man. And it is _as a man_ that you choose to help Deepwood: not a king claiming subjects, but a mortal who decides these people are worth saving. That choice is who you are when the god-power finally comes back — the throne returns, but the heart you earned as a human stays.

The trail ends at Deepwood.

## Deepwood
A once-thriving village, now almost empty. Its buildings stand **broken and ruined** — farm fallow, dock rotted, bank and school silent. A handful of survivors starve in the wreckage. Scattered through it are **the taken**: villagers frozen mid-motion, neither alive nor dead, pulsing faintly like a held breath. One still has enough of himself left to beg you for help.

You came to end an evil. Slowly you realize you've walked into its *home* — and ending it means saving every soul it holds first.

## The Enemy Wearing a Hero's Face: Orin
**Orin** appears as a stranded defender-mage — the last thing standing between Deepwood and the dark, hurling meteors at the nightly horde, dying for the village and rising each dawn to fight on. The survivors love him. **You fight beside him for most of the game.**

It is a mask. Orin **is the Monarch of Despair.** The horde is his; the "defense" is theater — a slow harvest dressed as heroism. He is genuinely **undying**: struck down, he collapses to an ember, reforms stronger, forever. You cannot out-damage a thing that treats death as sleep.

**Why he lets you live:** he thinks you are nobody — a stray adventurer clearing his dungeon, beneath killing. If you ever drew on your true Shadow Monarch power, he would recognize a rival monarch and destroy you instantly. **Your disguise is your survival.** (In fiction this is *why* the Shadow Monarch class is locked during the whole game — your power is sealed and hidden, and revealing it early would be death.)

**Why he can't die:** *an undivided soul cannot be destroyed.* Whole, he is beyond death. This is the lock on the entire game — and its key.

## The Shape of the Journey (how the story is played)
The story is *earned* through the core loop, not told in cutscenes:

1. **Descend.** Beneath Deepwood coils the dungeon where the evil nests — 100 levels down, each worse than the last, a boss guarding every fifth floor. You are reaching for the root.
2. **Free them.** Down there and in the ruins are the taken — named people with lives you uncover. Reawakening one is a light coming back on, not a checkbox.
3. **Rebuild.** Every soul you free can come home. Repair the broken buildings, put people back to the work that was theirs, let the village breathe again.
4. **Grow strong enough.** The village feeds you as you feed it — gold, materials, trained hands — and you turn that into power: skills, gear, a militia to hold the walls.
5. **The siege tightens.** As Deepwood heals, Orin's nightly assault escalates, because a *living* village is an insult to the Monarch of Despair. The endgame is the squeeze — every hour rescuing is an hour the walls stand undefended, and every hour on defense is a soul left frozen in the dark.

The village growing brighter and the nights growing worse are the same story told from both ends.

## The Reveal — at the gates of Level 100
You don't figure Orin out slowly. It comes in **two beats at the final threshold:**

1. **The shiver.** At the gate of Level 100, every enemy in the dungeon stops fighting *you* and turns to kneel before Orin, re-forming as *his* army. The mask falls — the defender was the Monarch of Despair all along. And in that instant something stirs in your sealed memory: not facts, a *flash* — you have faced this power before. *You have lost to it before.*
2. **The truth (after victory).** Only when you win and reclaim your form does the whole memory return: who you are, who he is, and the war that broke the world.

A short exchange passes between you at the gate — quiet, almost courteous, two monarchs recognizing each other at last. Then, with no buildup: **the hardest, wildest fight in the game, instantly.**

## The Climax: Divide the Soul
You cannot kill him — you've watched him prove it. So you force the one thing the undying never guard: you make him **split himself.** Cornered by a fully restored, fully defended Deepwood — the village he could never quite finish — the Monarch of Despair must shatter his soul across echoes of himself to survive the assault. *An undivided soul cannot be destroyed* — so you divide it. In the instant his soul is scattered and thin, the deathless thing is, for the first and only time, **mortal.** You strike the soul itself. Despair ends.

## The Return of the Shadow Monarch
In that final blow the seal breaks. Your power floods back, your memory with it — the throne, the name, the ancient war. **You reclaim your true Shadow Monarch form.** This is the endgame unlock: the **Shadow Monarch** class (the most powerful in the game), playable on replay — you, finally unmasked.

Deepwood stands alive, and it is yours to lead — but you lead it with a mortal's heart, not a conqueror's. One Monarch is destroyed. Somewhere beyond this world, the **Monarch of War** still waits. _(That reckoning is another story — a future game.)_
